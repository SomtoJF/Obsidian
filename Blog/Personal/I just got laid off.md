Surprise! surpriseee!!! I don't even know how to feel. I honestly liked the job. I felt like the company was moving in the right direction and that at least in the short term I was safe. 

> Boy was I wrong.

I was wrong not because the company wasn't moving in the right direction as an entity but because I forgot the supposed first rule about working in a startup. ==Don't get too comfortable==. 

The typical human response to situations like this is to look for who to point a finger at. Should I blame my bosses or should I blame myself? Believe me I nearly played that game. But I looked at things from the perspective of the former and realised I couldn't blame them. At least with my knowledge of the preceeding situation I couldn't. Maybe there was something I didn't know. _tbh there's probably a lot I don't know_. But I can't honestly dwell on that. Now should I blame myself? ==The most golden question to ever golden==. I honestly don't think I can. I was aware of the risks of working in a startup. Obv hearing the gist and having experience are two different things but still. I got questions from a few people asking why I still worked there. I had clear answers. For me, one principle I have always held is, it doesn't necessarily matter the direction you go (most times) as long as you have your reasons and can defend them. So I can't honestly put myself down too much. I know why I stayed. Again, I naïvely felt like if there were issues, I would be able to tell and execute a contingency plan. ==I admit, I have learned my lesson on that one==. Should I blame my bosses for not communicating the problem earlier?

Imagine you're the captain of a ship and there's a storm coming. Do you;
a. Alert everyone so _they get scared and panic_
b. Keep calm so _everyone keeps working_
c. Alert everyone so _they prepare their contingency plans_
d. Keep calm so _everyone stays relaxed and is taken by surprise_

I guess the answer depends on how bad the storm is. You can't control the second part of each of these options. The reactions. So we're back to two options. To speak or not to speak. But I did get a month and a half's worth of severance which I know isn't a lot but we (there was another engineer) were getting paid out of pocket so yh (copium :>). Maybe that's my alert?

I have an idea for what I need in my career right now. ==I need to work in a place where scale is a major problem==. The thing about working in a startup especially startups that are super early stage is that you often don't get to write code that affect hundreds of thousands or even millions of people simply because the product doesn't have that many users yet. Working at my (now former) company, I got to build so many interesting solutions, reverse engineer so many things and maybe the most valuable skill of all, I learned to write code that solves real world problems but I never really dealt with major scale. 

> I learned to write code that solves real world problems

The above item might seem like some cliché bullshit but honestly I mean it. So many times I received client call recordings where they complained about a problem and I would sit down to design and build a solution that solves that problem. Anyway, yeah I think that's it. Keyword here is ==SCALE==.
