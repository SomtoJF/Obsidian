### 1. Father vs. Uncle: Which is Better?

It is **significantly better and safer to get the gift deed from your father**.

Immigration officers evaluate the "plausibility" of a financial gift. In immigration assessment, parents gifting money to their children to start a new life abroad is recognized as standard, culturally normal, and widely accepted.

If you receive a large gift from an uncle, it raises immediate red flags for visa officers. IRCC officers often suspect that funds from extended family members may be borrowed money disguised as a gift to pass immigration checks, which must be repaid after you land in Canada. While a gift from an uncle is not legally prohibited, it triggers much higher scrutiny and requires an extensive paper trail proving a uniquely close relationship and the uncle's financial history.

### 2. Are Bank Statements Required from the Donor?

**Yes.** You will be required to provide your father’s bank statements alongside the gift deed.

IRCC requires proof of the **source of funds**. Providing a gift deed alone is not enough, because an applicant could theoretically have a relative sign a document while borrowing the money from an untraceable lender. Showing your father’s bank statements proves that:

- The money legitimately belonged to your father before it was transferred to you.
    
- He had the actual financial capacity to give you the gift.
    
- The funds were not taken as a quick loan right before being moved to your account.
    

### 3. What Documents You Need to Provide

To present an airtight gift deed package post-ITA, you will need to upload a bundle containing the following five elements:

#### A. Notarized Deed of Gift (Gift Deed)

A formal legal document drafted by a lawyer and signed/stamped before a **Notary Public** or **Commissioner for Oaths**. It must explicitly include:

- Full legal names, home addresses, and phone numbers of both the donor (father) and recipient (you).
    
- The exact monetary amount being gifted (expressed in NGN and CAD equivalent).
    
- A clear declaration of relationship (e.g., "I, [Father's Name], am the biological father of [Your Name]").
    
- An **irrevocability clause**: A statement that the money is an absolute, unconditional, non-repayable gift given out of love and affection, and that you are under no obligation to repay it at any point.
    
- The purpose of the gift (e.g., "To assist my child with settlement and living expenses upon immigrating to Canada as a Permanent Resident").
    

#### B. Donor’s Bank Statements (Source of Funds)

- **3 to 6 months of bank statements** from your father’s account.
    
- These statements should show how the money accumulated (e.g., routine business income, salary savings, fixed deposit liquidation, or asset sale).
    

#### C. Proof of Financial Transfer (Paper Trail)

- **Debit Advice / Transfer Receipt:** Official transaction receipt from your father's bank showing the money leaving his account.
    
- **Credit Advice / Bank Statement:** Your bank statement showing the exact matching inflow landing in your account.
    
- _Note:_ The transfer should ideally occur in a single, clean transaction rather than multiple piecemeal cash deposits.
    

#### D. Proof of Relationship

- A scanned copy of your official **Birth Certificate** (issued by the National Population Commission - NPC) displaying your full name and your father's full name, establishing the biological link.
    
- A copy of your father’s valid government-issued photo ID (e.g., Data page of International Passport or Driver’s License).
    

#### E. Applicant's Letter of Explanation (LoE)

A concise, 1-page summary letter written by you, positioned at the front of your proof of funds document package. In this letter:

- Detail the total liquid balance in your account.
    
- Explain that a portion (or all) of the funds was gifted to you by your father.
    
- Reference and index all attached exhibits (e.g., "Exhibit A: Notarized Gift Deed", "Exhibit B: Birth Certificate", "Exhibit C: Father's 6-Month Bank Statement", "Exhibit D: Transfer Receipts").
