First, a quick clarification on the phonetic symbols:

- **Closed sound $[\text{ø}]$:** Mouth mostly closed, lips tightly rounded (like making an "ee" sound with your tongue, but rounding your lips like you're going to whistle).
    
- **Open sound $[\text{œ}]$:** Mouth dropped open lower, lips relaxed and rounded (sounds very similar to the "u" sound in the English word _turn_ or _burn_).
    

## 1. The Core Rule: Open vs. Closed Syllables (La Règle de la Syllable)

The single most reliable rule for predicting whether **eu / œu** is open $[\text{œ}]$ or closed $[\text{ø}]$ depends on whether the sound lands in an **open syllable** or a **closed syllable**.

### A. Closed Sound $[\text{ø}]$ (In Open Syllables)

When **eu / œu** is at the **very end of a word or syllable** (meaning no spoken consonant follows it in that syllable), it is pronounced as the **closed $[\text{ø}]$**.

- **Feu** _(fire)_ $\rightarrow$ $/fø/$
    
- **Bleu** _(blue)_ $\rightarrow$ $/blø/$
    
- **Peu** _(little)_ $\rightarrow$ $/pø/$
    
- **Vœu** _(wish)_ $\rightarrow$ $/vø/$
    
- **Nœud** _(knot)_ $\rightarrow$ $/nø/$ _(The 'd' is silent, so the syllable ends in a vowel!)_
    
- **Jeux** _(games)_ $\rightarrow$ $/ʒø/$ _(The 'x' is silent!)_
    

### B. Open Sound $[\text{œ}]$ (In Closed Syllables)

When **eu / œu** is **followed by a pronounced consonant** in the same syllable (a closed syllable), your mouth naturally drops open, creating the **open $[\text{œ}]$**.

- **Fleur** _(flower)_ $\rightarrow$ $/flœʁ/$
    
- **Sœur** _(sister)_ $\rightarrow$ $/sœʁ/$
    
- **Cœur** _(heart)_ $\rightarrow$ $/kœʁ/$
    
- **Œuf** _(egg)_ $\rightarrow$ $/œf/$
    
- **Peur** _(fear)_ $\rightarrow$ $/pœʁ/$
    
- **Jeune** _(young)_ $\rightarrow$ $/ʒœn/$
    

## 2. Important Consonant Exceptions to the Syllable Rule

There are two major consonant exceptions where **eu / œu** remains **CLOSED $[\text{ø}]$**, even though a consonant sound follows it!

### Exception 1: Followed by the $[z]$ Sound

When **eu** is followed by a **z** sound (spelled with **z** or a single **s** between vowels), it is **always closed $[\text{ø}]$**.

- **Heureuse** _(happy, f)_ $\rightarrow$ $/ø-ʁøz/$
    
- **Neveuse** _(nervous, f)_ $\rightarrow$ $/nɛʁ-vøz/$
    
- **Creuse** _(hollow, f)_ $\rightarrow$ $/kʁøz/$
    

### Exception 2: Followed by the $[t]$ Sound (Spelled _-tre_ or _-t_)

When **eu** is followed by a **t** sound (in words ending in _-tre_ or certain _-t_ endings), it remains **closed $[\text{ø}]$**.

- **Neutre** _(neutral)_ $\rightarrow$ $/nøtʁ/$
    
- **Feutre** _(felt / marker)_ $\rightarrow$ $/føtʁ/$
    

## 3. Position in Multi-Syllable Words

### A. Non-Final Syllables (Unstressed)

When **eu** appears in the middle of a word prior to the final stressed syllable, it defaults to the **open sound $[\text{œ}]$** (or a neutral version of it) to allow for easier, faster speech.

- **Europe** $\rightarrow$ $/ø-ʁɔp/$ or $/œ-ʁɔp/$
    
- **Heureusement** _(fortunately)_ $\rightarrow$ $/ø-ʁœz-mɑ̃/$
    

## 4. The Famous Trap: The Verb _Avoir_

This is the most notorious spelling trap involving **eu** in the entire language.

In every form of the past participle and past simple of the verb **avoir** _(to have)_, the **eu** is NOT pronounced $[\text{ø}]$ or $[\text{œ}]$. **It is pronounced simply as a plain French "u" ($[y]$)**!

- **Eu** _(had)_ $\rightarrow$ Pronounced **/y/** (sounds identical to the letter **U**!).
    
- **J'ai eu** _(I had)_ $\rightarrow$ Pronounced _"zhay-OO"_ ($/\text{ʒe y}/$).
    
- **Nous eûmes** _(We had - past simple)_ $\rightarrow$ Pronounced $/nu \text{ ym}/$.
