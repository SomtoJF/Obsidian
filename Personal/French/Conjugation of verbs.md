## The Three Regular Verb Families

- **[[#-ER verb groups]]:** Regular verbs like _parler_ (to speak). Drop the _-er_ and add present endings: _-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent_. 
- **[[#IR Verb groups - (regular verbs)]]:** Regular verbs like _finir_ (to finish). Drop the _-ir_ and add present endings: _-is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent_. 
- **Third Group (-re / irregular):** Includes irregular verbs like _être_ (to be), _avoir_ (to have), and _aller_ (to go). These do not follow a single uniform pattern and must be memorized individually.

#### Important Heads-Up: The "Short" -IR Verbs
==Some very common -IR verbs like **partir** _(to leave)_, **servir** _(to serve)_, **sortir** _(to go out)_, and **dormir** _(to sleep)_ do **NOT** use the "-iss-" endings==. They form a small irregular subgroup where you drop the last consonant of the stem in the singular:
- **Je dors** (from _dormir_)
- **Je pars** (from _partir_)
- **Je sors** (from _sortir_)

**There is no rule on the word itself that tells you if an -IR verb is regular or irregular.** You can't look at the infinitive alone and know for sure. However, you can rely on **pattern recognition** because the vast majority of -IR verbs belong to the regular group.

|**Pronoun**|**Stem + Ending**|**Spoken Pronunciation**|
|---|---|---|
|**Je**|dor**s**|`/doʁ/` (Silent **-s**)|
|**Tu**|dor**s**|`/doʁ/` (Silent **-s**)|
|**Il / Elle / On**|dor**t**|`/doʁ/` (Silent **-t**)|
|**Nous**|dorm**ons**|`/doʁ-mɔ̃/`|
|**Vous**|dorm**ez**|`/doʁ-may/`|
|**Ils / Elles**|dorm**ent**|`/doʁm/` (Silent **-ent**, but you hear the **m**!)|
##### The Big 4 Short -IR Verbs

These four all share this exact same trick:

|**Infinitive**|**Je / Tu / Il (Singular)**|**Nous / Vous / Ils (Plural)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Dormir** _(to sleep)_|Je dor**s** / Il dor**t**|Nous dorm**ons** / Ils dorm**ent**|
|**Partir** _(to leave)_|Je par**s** / Il par**t**|Nous part**ons** / Ils part**ent**|
|**Sortir** _(to go out)_|Je sor**s** / Il sor**t**|Nous sort**ons** / Ils sort**ent**|
|**Servir** _(to serve)_|Je ser**s** / Il ser**t**|Nous serv**ons** / Ils serv**ent**|
### RE verb groups
To conjugate a regular -RE verb:
1. Chop off the **-re** to find your stem (e.g., _vendre_ $\rightarrow$ **vend-**).
2. Add the present tense endings: **-s, -s, (nothing!), -ons, -ez, -ent**.

> 💡 **Notice the Il/Elle form:** Unlike -ER (_-e_) or -IR (_-t_), regular -RE verbs add **no ending at all** for _il/elle/on_. You simply leave the stem ending in **d**!

#### 1. _Vendre_ (To Sell)

Stem: **vend-**

| **Pronoun**        | **Ending** | **Written Form** | **Spoken Pronunciation**                            |
| ------------------ | ---------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Je**             | **-s**     | Je vend**s**     | `/vɑ̃/` (Silent **-ds**)                            |
| **Tu**             | **-s**     | Tu vend**s**     | `/vɑ̃/` (Silent **-ds**)                            |
| **Il / Elle / On** | _(none)_   | On ven**d**      | `/vɑ̃/` (Silent **-d**)                             |
| **Nous**           | **-ons**   | Nous vend**ons** | `/vɑ̃-dɔ̃/`                                         |
| **Vous**           | **-ez**    | Vous vend**ez**  | `/vɑ̃-day/`                                         |
| **Ils / Elles**    | **-ent**   | Ils vend**ent**  | `/vɑ̃d/` (Silent **-ent**, but you hear the **d**!) |
**Pronunciation Keys:**
- **Je vends**, **tu vends**, and **il vend** are all pronounced identically (`/vɑ̃/`). The **d** and **s** at the end are silent.
- In **ils vendent**, the **-ent** is silent, but it "wakes up" the **d** sound at the end of the stem (`/vɑ̃d/`).

#### Attendre (To wait)
- J'attends _-remember silent $ds$_
- Tu attends
- Il/Elle/On attend
- Nous attendons
- Vous attendez
- Ils/Elles attendent
**Note on Meaning:** _Attendre_ means =="to wait for"== built-in! So "I am waiting for the bus" is simply **_J'attends le bus_** (no extra preposition needed).

#### Other Common Regular -RE Verbs
Once you know _vendre_, you can instantly conjugate all of these:
- **Rendre** _(to return / give back)_ $\rightarrow$ Je rends, il rend, nous rendons
- **Perdre** _(to lose)_ $\rightarrow$ Je me perds, tu me perds, il perd
- **Répondre (à)** _(to answer)_ $\rightarrow$ Je réponds, il répond
- **Entendre** _(to hear)_ $\rightarrow$ J'entends, tu entends

## Key insights
- In spoken French, the singular forms—==**Je**, **Tu**, and **Il / Elle / On**—sound **100% identical**== across almost every single verb in the language!
- Even though written French forces you to spell them with different endings (**-e/-es/-e**, **-is/-is/-it**, or **-s/-s/-d**), ==spoken French ignores those trailing consonants entirely==.
